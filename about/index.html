About

I’m Nikhil, a software engineer based in Washington, D.C. Most of my attention goes to AI, infrastructure, and the parts of a system that only reveal themselves under load — schedulers, queues, container runtimes, and the failure modes hiding behind them.

Before that I completed my MS in Computer Science at George Washington University, which is where most of my systems background comes from: distributed systems, machine learning, and the long tail of coursework that turns out to matter later.

I learn by building, so a lot of my work lives outside of a job description. TaskFlow is a distributed task queue with chaos engineering built in — inject broker failures and worker crashes, then see whether exactly-once delivery actually holds. K8s Autopilot is a multi-tenant Kubernetes platform with an operator that autoscales and heals workloads per tenant. ObserveX and Heapify came out of the same habit: pick something I only half understand, then build it until I do.

I write about what I learn — Linux, containers, concurrency models, the occasional piece of number theory — mostly to check that I actually understood the thing rather than just recognised it. What I’m reading alongside that is on my bookshelf, papers included.

Away from the terminal I walk the city with a camera, collecting monuments at night, campus corners, and the occasional duck.

The best way to get in touch with me is through email or LinkedIn. Everything else is over here.
